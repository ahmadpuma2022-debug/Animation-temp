# What if you dug down and couldn't stop? — journey (27 stages, 8:02)

YouTube check (yt-dlp search, 2026-11-27): the most-viewed video with this exact idea is "What If You Dug a Hole Through the Earth and Couldn't Stop?" with 39,312 views (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=94hPPYZPIh0). Under the 500,000 limit, so the idea is open.

Length: 8:02 = 482 s. Last stage holds from 7:45 to 8:02.

Big number (left): **distance from your room**, in a straight line. It equals depth until the Earth's centre (6,371 km), then keeps growing as you climb out the other side, up to 12,742 km, the full width of the planet.

Top-right number: **rock temperature** (seawater temperature in the last 4 stages).
- Down to 12.3 km: a straight-line model, 12 °C + 13.7 °C per km. It reproduces the measured 180 °C at the bottom of the Kola borehole, and gives about 65 °C at 3.9 km (a real deep gold mine is about 60 °C).
- Below that: estimates from published Earth models. Real values are uncertain, so the readout should show them rounded, as written here.

## Estimates and weak points
- Stages 1–5 depths and temperatures, the seabed-to-surface temperatures (stages 23, 25, 26) and the sea-surface temperature are estimates.
- All temperatures below 12.3 km are model estimates, not measurements. Real values vary by place.
- The 13.7 °C/km line is a fit to Kola. The Mponeng temperature (about 60 °C) is a little under it (65 °C).
- Kola is the deepest vertical borehole. The Chayvo Z-44 well is longer (12,376 m), but along a slanted path. Line 11 therefore says "deeper than this" about vertical depth.
- Stage 23 assumes you come out under an ocean, which is true for most places but not all. A different start point would change stages 23–26.
